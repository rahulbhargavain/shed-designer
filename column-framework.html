v5.0 Asymmetric Corner Optimization

(1) 2 Double + 2 Single Corners: Reduced corner double angles from 4 down to 2 diagonally opposite corners (e.g. TL and BR). The remaining two corners (TR and BL) are single angles, saving 12.3 kg of steel.
(2) No 450mm Face Stringer Plates: Central stringers are single angles without plate reinforcement, saving 9.6 kg of steel. The short span between yokes keeps bending stress to just 30% of single-angle capacity.
(3) Symmetric Ply Joints: Double-angle corners allow sandwich clamping at opposite corners, while single-angle corners are bolted normally.
(4) Total Weight Reduced to ~139 kg: Saves 22 kg over v4.0 and 60 kg over v3.0, representing a massive cost and material optimization.

2. Asymmetric Corners — Structural Rationale

By using diagonally opposite double-angle corners (e.g. TL and BR) and single-angle corners (TR and BL), we preserve structural symmetry and sandwich clamping capacity where panels meet, while saving 12.3 kg of steel angles.

Corner Clamping Details

Double-angle corners are used at TL and BR, allowing standard double-angle sandwich clamping. At TR and BL, a single angle is placed on the outside of the corner. Ply panels are screwed or bolted directly through the legs of the single angle, which provides identical boundary stiffness at 25% stress levels.

3. Yoke Bending Moments & Type Selection

Yokes are spaced from 0 mm (bottom) to 2438 mm (top). The tributary heights are used to compute the UDL and bending moments on the yokes. Single angles are used unless the moment exceeds 246 N·m, where double angles are required.

5. Step-by-Step Build Schedule

1
Fabrication: Cut angles and plate stiffeners

Cut 8 pieces of ISA 35×5 angle to 2438 mm (8 ft) for vertical members. Cut 26 yoke longs (550 mm) and 26 yoke shorts (400 mm). Pre-cut two 75×5 mm flat bars to 2438 mm. Drill M12 holes in angles and plates.

2
Weld diagonally opposite corner verticals

Welding: Weld 2 corner double-angle pairs (TL and BR) back-to-back, ≥ 5mm fillet at 150mm pitch. The TR and BL corners, and the central 450mm face stringers, remain single angles (no plates required).

3
Attach vertical 75×5 mm flat bars to yokes

The two 75×5 mm vertical flat bars are welded directly along the centerline of each 300mm face yokes. Weld them securely at each yoke crossing. This allows the flat bar to act as a continuous vertical beam supporting the 300mm face ply.

4
Assemble 450mm face plywood panels

Sandwich-bolt 20-inch (508mm) ply panels to central stringers and double corner angles on the 450mm faces. M12 bolts at ≤ 250mm pitch. Ensure tight bearing contact. Apply form release agent.

5
Fit yokes from BASE to TOP (0 to 2438 mm)

Erect panels, install yokes at exact heights:
• Yoke 1 (0 mm - base) — Single angle — M16 Gr 8.8 bolts
• Yoke 2 (200 mm) — Double angle — M16 Gr 8.8 bolts
• Yoke 3 (420 mm) — Double angle — M16 Gr 8.8 bolts
• Yoke 4 (660 mm) — Double angle — M16 Gr 8.8 bolts
• Yoke 5 (920 mm) — Double angle — M16 Gr 8.8 bolts
• Yoke 6 (1200 mm) — Single angle — M12 Gr 4.6 bolts
• Yoke 7 (1500 mm) — Single angle — M12 Gr 4.6 bolts
• Yoke 8 (1870 mm) — Single angle — M12 Gr 4.6 bolts
• Yoke 9 (2438 mm - top) — Single angle — M12 Gr 4.6 bolts

6
Pre-pour checks & M25 concrete pour

Verify all 9 yokes are aligned and bolted. Ensure the 75×5 mm flat bar stiffeners bear firmly against the 300mm ply panels. Verify diagonal braces. Pour concrete in lifts ≤ 500 mm.
